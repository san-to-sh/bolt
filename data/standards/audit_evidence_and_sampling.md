# Synthetic Audit Standard — Sufficient Appropriate Audit Evidence and Sampling

## S1. Sufficient Appropriate Audit Evidence

The auditor's conclusions must be supported by evidence that is both **sufficient** (enough in quantity) and **appropriate** (relevant and reliable in quality). Evidence obtained directly by the auditor is generally more reliable than evidence provided by the entity. External evidence is generally more reliable than internal evidence. Documentary evidence is generally more reliable than oral representations.

The auditor uses professional judgment to determine whether enough evidence has been obtained to support the conclusion. If the available evidence is insufficient, the auditor must perform additional procedures or qualify the conclusion.

## S2. Audit Sampling

When it is impractical to examine every item in a population, the auditor selects a sample and projects the results to the population. The sample must be representative — every item in the population should have a chance of being selected.

Common sampling approaches include:

- **Random sampling** — each item has an equal probability of selection; suitable for attribute testing where all items carry similar risk.
- **Monetary-unit sampling (MUS)** — probability of selection is proportional to the item's recorded amount; gives greater coverage to larger items and is commonly used for substantive testing of account balances.
- **Haphazard sampling** — items selected without a structured method but without conscious bias; acceptable for low-risk populations but less defensible than statistical methods.

## S3. Evaluating Sample Results

After testing the selected items, the auditor projects the findings to the full population. If deviations or misstatements are found in the sample, the auditor considers whether the projected rate or amount exceeds the tolerable threshold.

The auditor also considers whether the nature of any exceptions suggests a systemic issue rather than an isolated error, and whether additional testing or an expanded sample is warranted.

## S4. Documentation

The auditor documents the sampling method used, the population from which the sample was drawn, the items selected, the results of testing each item, and the conclusion reached. This documentation supports the reproducibility and reviewability of the audit procedure.
