# Synthetic Audit Standard — Financial Statement Assertions

## S1. What Are Assertions

Assertions are the implicit or explicit claims made by management about the transactions, balances, and disclosures in the financial statements. Auditors design procedures to test whether these claims hold true. Each audit procedure targets one or more specific assertions.

## S2. Transaction-Level Assertions

- **Occurrence** — Transactions and events that have been recorded actually took place and pertain to the entity. A payment recorded in the ledger should correspond to a real purchase that the entity made.

- **Completeness** — All transactions and events that should have been recorded have in fact been recorded. Nothing is missing from the population.

- **Authorization** — All transactions were approved by personnel with the appropriate authority to do so, in accordance with the entity's delegation-of-authority policies.

- **Accuracy** — Amounts and other data relating to recorded transactions have been recorded correctly, in the right accounts, and at the right values.

- **Cutoff** — Transactions have been recorded in the correct accounting period. Revenue and expenses are recognized in the period in which they were earned or incurred, not shifted between periods.

## S3. Balance-Level Assertions

- **Existence** — Assets, liabilities, and equity interests exist at the reporting date.

- **Rights and obligations** — The entity holds or controls the rights to its assets, and liabilities represent genuine obligations.

- **Valuation and allocation** — Assets, liabilities, and equity are recorded at appropriate amounts, and any resulting valuation or allocation adjustments are properly recorded.

## S4. Linking Assertions to Procedures

Each audit procedure should clearly state which assertion(s) it addresses. For example, vouching a recorded payment to a supporting purchase order tests the **occurrence** and **authorization** assertions. Scanning for unrecorded liabilities tests the **completeness** assertion.
